---
title: Spacetime Objects
---

You can place several different types of entities in a spacetime drawing:

## 1. Events

An **Event** represents a single point in space and time $(x, t)$.

* **In Spacetime Diagram**: Drawn as a dot.

* **In Observer View**: Appears at the exact time the event occurs and slowly
  fades out as time progresses.

## 2. Light Cones

A **Light Cone** represents the path of light emitted from a single event.

* **In Spacetime Diagram**: Drawn as a V-shape extending upward and outward at
  45-degree angles (representing the speed of light $c = 1.0$).

* **In Observer View**: Appears as expanding wavefronts moving outward from the
  emission point.

If you switch from the Lorentz view to the emitter or ether view, then the
light cone might not be at a 45-degree angle — the speed might not be 1.0 in the
current observer's reference frame.

## 3. Instants (Planes of Simultaneity)

An **Instant** represents a set of coordinates that an observer considers to
be at the same time.

* **In Spacetime Diagram**: Drawn as a tilted line representing the observer's
  plane of simultaneity.

* **In Observer View**: Represents the spatial slice shown at the selected time.

If you switch from the Lorentz view to the emitter or ether view, then an
instant is a horizontal line in the main view.

## 4. Locations (Worldlines)

**Locations** are entities that persist over time, represented by their
worldlines. You can choose from different sprites:

* **Person**: A walking figure.

* **Train**: A moving train compartment. Useful for visualizing Einstein's
  train-and-platform experiments.

* **Barn**: A stationary or moving barn structure. Useful for the
  "ladder/train in barn" paradox.

* **Flag**: A marker flag.

* **Clock**: Displays the proper time elapsed along that worldline.

Clocks and other location sprites automatically reflect length contraction and
time dilation in the Side View based on their relative speed to the current
observer frame.
